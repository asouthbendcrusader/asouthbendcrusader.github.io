§01 — The Man

Michael G. "Mike" Schalk is President of the Roseland Town Council. Under Indiana law (IC 36-5-2-2), a town council is the town's legislative body and its president is the town executive. Roseland has no mayor. Schalk is the town government.

He won the seat at the ballot box: on November 7, 2023, he was elected to the council at-large with 47 votes, the top vote-getter in the race (Official St. Joseph County 2023 Municipal General Election results). On January 11, 2024, after a fellow council member said she did not recall a vote ever being taken, Schalk moved to ratify his own election as president. The motion passed 2–0 with one abstention, on the advice of Town Attorney Pete Agostino (Town of Roseland minutes, January 11, 2024).

Before the council, Schalk worked for the town: Roseland's maintenance foreman (Roseland payroll records, 2016). On the May 2026 recording below, Schalk himself states he was fired from that department.

On May 15, 2026, WSBT reported residents alleging town council corruption at the May 14 meeting. Schalk publicly denied the claims and denied owning Advanced Property Maintenance (APM); he acknowledged a friendship with APM's owner and past work for the company, and said he filed a conflict-of-interest disclosure. The APM contracts, invoices, and payments, and the disclosure itself, are the subject of pending records requests. None of that paper has been produced.

§02 — Never an Officer

On October 29, 2026, a Report of Cause concerning Schalk was filed with the Indiana Law Enforcement Academy. The Academy answered in writing the same day, at 1:07 PM:

Indiana Law Enforcement Academy — Written Determination, October 29, 2026
"Mr. Schalk is not currently, and has never been an officer in the State of Indiana. Therefore, we have no authority over him whatsoever. We consider this matter closed on our end."
Anna Kingsley, Paralegal, Indiana Law Enforcement Academy (ILEALegal@ilea.in.gov), October 29, 2026. The Academy's lane is closed on jurisdiction, because he was never an officer. The letter is not a ruling on anything Schalk did; it establishes the fact this whole file turns on: the man who runs Roseland's police department has never held any law-enforcement authority in Indiana.

§05 — Town Hall, October 29, 2026

On the morning of October 29, 2026, the 13-page Report of Cause on Officer Shawn Apps was hand-delivered to Schalk at the Roseland Town Hall counter, at 9:40 AM. Before a second filing could be presented, Schalk gave an order. His exact words:

The Order — Roseland Town Hall Counter, October 29, 2026
"You need to turn your phone off right here, and you can go ahead and leave, because I'm not gonna discuss personnel issues."
Confirmed word-for-word by the man who was standing in the room. The order was complied with, on camera, and every remaining filing went in on paper the same day.
Schalk at the Town Hall counter on October 29, 2026, holding the hand-delivered Report of Cause. Still from the delivery video, the compiler's own recording.

Schalk carried a gun on his hip at that counter. That is stated here on three separate feet: the compiler's own firsthand observation in that room; the confirmation of senior Roseland officers, one of whom has given his word on the record that he will testify Schalk carries a .38 on his hip; and Schalk's own written receipt below, confirming he was the official at that counter at 9:40 that morning. In fairness to the whole record: the delivery video itself does not show a firearm in any frame, and the carry, standing alone, is in all likelihood lawful under Indiana law. The gun is not the charge.

The charge is the order. A citizen standing at a public counter, in the middle of filing public records, was ordered to stop recording and leave a public building, by a man the Indiana Law Enforcement Academy confirms has never held any law-enforcement authority in his life. A criminal complaint asking the St. Joseph County Prosecutor to evaluate that conduct was filed the same day. On October 30, the Prosecutor's office answered that it "does not conduct criminal investigations" and referred the matter to the Indiana State Police, where the full package was filed that day. No charging decision has been made by anyone.

§06 — The Receipt, and the Wall

Schalk would not sign a receipt at the counter. At 12:29 PM that same day, he confirmed the delivery in writing himself:

Schalk — Written Receipt, October 29, 2026, 12:29 PM
"Yes, we received your hand delivery of the Report of cause for Revocation, on this Date of October 29, 2026, at 9:40am!"
In the same reply, Schalk announced the new rules for the public: all future information requests must be made on the Town's signed paper form only, no email requests; copies at $.10 a page; and video footage "can be charged up to $150.00." The preservation demand covering the Town Hall surveillance video of that morning (9:25–9:55 AM) was already pending when the price was posted. The Town's written response is due November 12, 2026.

§07 — The 1985 Docket, and the Question It Leaves Open

In Montgomery Circuit Court in Crawfordsville, Indiana, there is a public docket. It reads:

CaseState of Indiana v. Schalk, Mike — CR1985-18, Montgomery Circuit Court
FiledFebruary 28, 1985
ChargeRobbery — Felony, Class B
JudgmentFinding of Guilty
January 9, 1990Judgment entered; Department of Correction Certificate of Discharge filed the same day
ExpungementNo expungement marking appears anywhere on the public docket

Now hear the man himself, from the May 20, 2026 recording. On it, Schalk says he was arrested "down in Crawfordsville," the county seat of Montgomery County, and he says, more than once, that his conviction was expunged: "that was before I got my record expunged" (00:08:38); "it's expunged" (00:08:50).

Asked for the CR1985-18 file, the Montgomery County Clerk's office answered on October 30, 2026, in one line:

Montgomery County Clerk's Office — October 30, 2026
"We are not able to give out any information on expunged cases."
That is the clerk's office treating the case as an expunged case. It is not an expungement order, and no order has been produced. A statewide court search on October 31, 2026 found no expungement case under Schalk's name, with this caveat: a sealed expungement case would not appear in that search by design.

Why one piece of paper decides everything: under Indiana law (IC 3-8-1-5), a felony conviction disqualifies a person from holding elected office, with no time limit. But an expunged conviction is excepted (IC 3-8-1-5(c)(2)(E)), and expungement expressly restores the right to hold public office (IC 35-38-9-10(c)). If the expungement is real, Schalk is fully eligible and this file will print that. If it is not, the President of the Roseland Town Council is holding a seat the law does not allow him to hold.

Everything in this section turns on one document: an expungement order. It has never been produced. On October 31, 2026, the Montgomery County Clerk was asked in writing to state whether one exists, and the St. Joseph County Election Board was asked for Schalk's complete candidate file, including the declarations about his own record that he signed when he ran. Both answers are outstanding. When the paper arrives, it goes on this page, whichever way it cuts.
